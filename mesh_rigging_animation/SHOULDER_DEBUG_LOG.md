# Shoulder Rotation Debugging Log

## Problem Statement

When viewing the BVH output in Blender for `Calibration_trial_4_Douglas_04-03_rotations.c3d`,
shoulder elevation appeared sign-inverted specifically when the arm is pointing forward
(`shoulder_horiz_corr ≈ +89–90 deg`):

- Frame 3053 (movement 1): `shoulder_abd_corr = +8.5 deg` → skeleton arm goes DOWN (expected UP)
- Frame 3118 (movement 2): `shoulder_abd_corr = -3.86 deg` → skeleton arm goes UP (expected DOWN)

Movements 3 and 4 (frames 4245–4340, wrist flexion/extension) appear approximately correct.

**Root cause confirmed (see BVH Axis Probe section): this is a gimbal lock artifact, not
a sign error. The current sign conventions are correct. The BVH ZYX representation breaks
down when horiz ≈ 90 deg, causing ABD changes to manifest as axial (palm) rotation rather
than elevation.**

---

## Current Pipeline Overview

**File:** `c3d_to_bvh.py`

Cometa C3D files store pre-computed anatomical joint angles at 2000 Hz (stride-sampled
to the true IMU rate of ~142.857 Hz). The pipeline:

1. Reads shoulder/elbow/wrist angle channels from the ANALOG section
2. Applies T-pose offset corrections (mean of first 1.0 s subtracted from abd, vert, dev)
3. Applies chest IMU quaternion T-pose calibration (left-multiply: `q_tpose^-1 * q_raw`)
4. Maps angles to BVH ZYX Euler channels via `_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()` (shoulder),
   `_spherical_to_zyx()` (elbow, wrist), and `_quat_to_zyx()` (chest/Spine1)
5. Writes CMU mocap full-body BVH skeleton (Y-up, Z-forward, right arm at -X at T-pose)

### Shoulder Euler Sequence

`_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()` reconstructs the full rotation matrix using scipy:

```python
R = Rotation.from_euler('ZXY', [abd_sign*abd, vert_sign*vert, horiz_sign*horiz], degrees=True)
tz, ty, tx = R.as_euler('ZYX', degrees=True)
return z_sign * tz, y_sign * ty, x_sign * tx
```

The docstring justification (Henschke et al. 2022, PMC9364332):
- Cometa uses intrinsic **XZ'Y''** in their anatomical frame
- Mapped to BVH world axes (Y-up, Z-forward, right arm at -X): sequence becomes **ZX'Y''**
  - Z (1st) = Abduction/Adduction (`shoulder_abd`) — coronal axis
  - X' (2nd) = Vertical Flex/Ext (`shoulder_vert`) — humerus axial rotation
  - Y'' (3rd) = Horizontal Flex/Ext (`shoulder_horiz`) — sagittal axis

## Attempted Fixes and Outcomes

### 1. `abd_sign` flip: `abd_sign=1.0` → `-1.0` — REJECTED (caused regressions)

Changing the sign applied to the raw ABD angle before Euler reconstruction couples into
the `vert` and `horiz` channels via the rotation matrix, introducing errors in other
movements. The user confirmed this caused visible regressions. NOT a valid fix.

### 2. `z_sign` flip: `z_sign=-1.0` → `+1.0` — CONFIRMED INCORRECT

The BVH axis probe (see below) established that:
- Positive BVH Zrotation → arm moves DOWN
- Negative BVH Zrotation → arm moves UP

With `z_sign = -1.0`, positive ABD produces negative theta_z, which raises the arm UP.
This is correct. `probe_key_frames.bvh` confirmed: "Pure ABD +30" shows arm raising UP
with the current `z_sign = -1.0`. Changing to `+1.0` would invert the pure-ABD elevation.

### 3. Chest quaternion RIGHT multiplication — NOT THE CAUSE

For movements 1–4, the chest is static (W ≈ 0.999). LEFT vs RIGHT multiplication
produces only minor differences (all non-W components < 0.07). Not the primary cause.

## Root Cause: Gimbal Lock at horiz ≈ 90 deg

### Why gimbal lock occurs in BVH ZYX

BVH uses ZYX Euler angles, meaning the physical rotation applied is:

```
R_final = Rz(tz) * Ry(ty) * Rx(tx)   [applied globally, right-to-left]
```

The middle angle `ty` (Yrotation) produces a singularity when `ty = ±90 deg`. At that
point, the arm points along the ±Z axis (forward/backward). Geometrically, once the arm
is along Z, rotating further around Z (what Rz does) spins the arm around its own length
axis — this is axial rotation, not elevation.

The shoulder has `ty ≈ horiz_deg` in the BVH output. When `horiz ≈ 90 deg`, the ZYX
representation is at its singularity. Small ABD changes near this position get absorbed
into an axial spin (Zrotation around the arm axis) rather than a vertical elevation change.

### What this means for movements 1 and 2

At frames 3053–3118, `shoulder_horiz ≈ +89–90 deg`. In the ZYX representation:
- `ty ≈ +89.5 to +90 deg` — at or near the gimbal lock limit
- A change of ABD from +8.5 to -3.86 deg (a -12.36 deg swing) should produce a small
  elevation decrease, but instead the ZYX decomposition converts it into a palms-down
  axial rotation, because `Rz` at `ty = 90 deg` acts along the arm's own axis.

The original observation ("arm DOWN for movement 1, arm UP for movement 2") was a
misidentification of this axial rotation artifact as an elevation change. The sign
conventions are correct. The representation itself is broken at this arm position.

## Research Findings: Established Solutions for Shoulder Gimbal Lock

The following is based on literature research conducted 2026-03-08.

### Why ISB XZY (Cometa's source format) is not the problem

Cometa WaveTrack uses the ISB-recommended intrinsic **XZ'Y''** (XZY) sequence for the
shoulder (confirmed by Henschke et al. 2022, PMC9364332 and the ISB 2005 standard,
Wu et al., J Biomech 38:981-992). The singularity of XZY occurs when the **middle angle
(Z) = ±90 deg**, which anatomically corresponds to 90 deg of pure coronal-plane abduction
(arm out to the side). Critically:

- At arm-forward (90 deg forward flexion): XZY is **singularity-free**. The Z middle
  angle stays small; only the X (first) angle is large. Multiple studies confirm XZY
  handles sagittal-plane flexion correctly at all angles up to 90 deg.
- Senk & Cheze (2006) tested 12 shoulder postures and found XZY was **"the only
  decomposition not to suffer from gimbal lock"** across a broad range of shoulder
  movements including overhead athletic motions.
  (PMC3042544, doi:10.1016/j.jbiomech.2005.06.004)

### Why the conversion to BVH ZYX introduces a new singularity

BVH ZYX has its singularity at **middle angle (Y) = ±90 deg**. In the BVH world frame,
this Y angle encodes horizontal flexion (horiz). When `horiz ≈ 90 deg` (arm pointing
forward), the ZYX decomposition collapses — `Rz` and `Rx` become coupled, and small
changes in abduction (ABD) produce axial (palm) rotation instead of elevation change.

This singularity does **not** exist in the Cometa/ISB XZY source data. It is introduced
entirely by the conversion step `R.as_euler('ZYX')` in `_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()`.

```
Source (Cometa XZY):   singularity at 90 deg abduction  -- rarely reached in therapy
BVH ZYX (current):     singularity at 90 deg flexion    -- commonly reached in therapy
```

### Established fix: use XZY channel order for shoulder BVH channels

The research-backed solution (multiple papers, MotionBuilder documentation, NASA IK
artifacts paper) is to **keep the BVH shoulder channel order as XZY**, matching the ISB
source convention. BVH format explicitly supports per-joint channel order via the
CHANNELS declaration. This:

1. Preserves the source singularity structure (at 90 deg abduction, where the source
   data already cannot represent the pose correctly anyway)
2. Removes the arm-forward singularity entirely
3. Requires no intermediate virtual joints
4. The decomposition becomes `R.as_euler('XZY')` instead of `R.as_euler('ZYX')`

Note: our earlier "Option B" proposed ZXY, which was not research-backed. The correct
order is **XZY**, which matches ISB and is the sequence empirically validated to avoid
gimbal lock across the broadest range of shoulder ROM.

### Earlier options reassessed

**Option A (virtual RightShoulder joint):** Valid as a rigging technique (used in Maya,
MotionBuilder "twist bone" workflows), but unnecessary when the channel-order fix is
available. Adds complexity for no additional benefit in this case.

**Option B (ZXY channel order):** Our original proposal before research. ZXY was found
to be the **worst** sequence for shoulder abduction-dominant motions (Senk & Cheze 2006).
Not recommended.

**Confirmed fix (XZY channel order):** Research-backed, minimal change, preserves source
data's singularity position.

## Implementation Plan: XZY Channel Order for Shoulder

### Files to change

**`bvh_writer.py`** — change CHANNELS declaration for `RightArm` and `LeftArm` only:
```
# Before:
CHANNELS 3 Zrotation Yrotation Xrotation

# After (shoulder arm joints only):
CHANNELS 3 Xrotation Zrotation Yrotation
```

`RightForeArm`, `RightHand`, `LeftForeArm`, `LeftHand` use `_spherical_to_zyx()` which
is a separate conversion path — their channel order change requires independent
re-validation of elbow and wrist sign conventions. Scope those separately.

**`c3d_to_bvh.py` → `_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()`** — change output decomposition:
```python
# Before:
tz, ty, tx = R.as_euler('ZYX', degrees=True)
return z_sign * tz, y_sign * ty, x_sign * tx   # written as Z Y X

# After:
tx, tz, ty = R.as_euler('XZY', degrees=True)
return x_sign * tx, z_sign * tz, y_sign * ty   # written as X Z Y
```

The sign variables (z_sign, y_sign, x_sign) retain their physical meaning; only the
output order changes to match the new channel declaration.

**`c3d_to_bvh.py` → `_map_angles_to_bvh()` and `write_bvh_from_joint_angles()`** — the
`arm_str` is already written as a 3-float string; no order change needed there since
`_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()` returns values in the correct channel order.

### What needs re-validation after the change

- **RightArm / LeftArm elevation and horizontal flexion**: load `probe_key_frames.bvh`
  equivalent after the change and confirm arm UP for positive ABD, arm FORWARD for
  positive horiz
- **Axial rotation (shoulder_vert / x_sign)**: verify palms-backward is still positive
- **Elbow and wrist**: no change to their conversion path — no re-validation needed

## XZY Implementation Attempt and Revert (2026-03-08)

### What was implemented

The XZY channel order fix was applied to three files:

1. `bvh_writer.py`: RightArm and LeftArm CHANNELS changed from `Zrotation Yrotation Xrotation`
   to `Xrotation Zrotation Yrotation`.
2. `c3d_to_bvh.py` (`_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()`): Decomposition changed from
   `R.as_euler('ZYX')` to `R.as_euler('XZY')`, return order changed to `(x_sign*tx, z_sign*tz, y_sign*ty)`.
3. `bvh_axis_probe.py`: `_arm_line()` and callers updated to write in (X, Z, Y) order.

Mathematical verification of the new output showed no gimbal lock warnings from scipy.
Probe key frames showed clean values: Movement 1 tz_out=-8.500, ty_out=89.500;
Movement 2 tz_out=+3.860, ty_out=90.000. Sign directions appeared correct.

### Why it was reverted

When the regenerated C3D animation was loaded in Blender, the overall shoulder motion
was less accurate than the ZYX baseline — not just the gimbal lock frames, but the
general shoulder motion quality degraded. The reason for this regression is not fully
understood. Hypotheses:

- The sign conventions (z_sign, y_sign, x_sign) were empirically calibrated against the
  ZYX decomposition. Under XZY, the coupling between decomposed angles changes for combined
  ABD + horizontal flex poses, and the same sign values may not correctly map the physical
  motion across the full ROM.
- Blender's BVH interpolation between keyframes may behave differently for XZY vs ZYX
  channel order, producing unexpected motion arcs even when individual poses are correct.

Both files were reverted. Current state: `c3d_to_bvh.py` and `bvh_writer.py` are both
back to ZYX. The pipeline is internally consistent.

### Current status and open questions

The gimbal lock diagnosis at horiz=90 deg is confirmed (scipy warning, probe artifact).
The XZY fix is theoretically correct but caused empirical regression when applied.

**Open questions requiring research:**

1. Is Blender's BVH importer known to handle non-ZYX channel orders correctly, or does
   it silently reorder channels? (Would explain regression without a data bug.)
2. Are there existing libraries or pipelines that specifically handle Cometa WaveTrack
   C3D (pre-computed anatomical angles, no marker positions) to BVH conversion?
3. The Cometa format is unusual: it stores ISB-standard joint angles, not spatial markers.
   Standard C3D-to-BVH tools assume marker data. Has the angular-only C3D-to-BVH problem
   been solved elsewhere?

### Architecture note

The shoulder conversion is fully isolated from all other joints:
- Chest uses `_quat_to_zyx()` independently
- Elbow and wrist use `_spherical_to_zyx()` independently
- Any future fix to shoulder only requires changes to `_euler_shoulder_to_zyx()` and
  the two CHANNELS declarations (RightArm, LeftArm) in `bvh_writer.py`.
